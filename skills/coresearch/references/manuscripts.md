# Manuscripts, review, and release

Writing should expose the actual contribution and its evidence, not upgrade the
claim beyond the study. Work directly in the user's existing draft and format.
For explanations, connect concepts to mechanisms and assumptions rather than
adding a separate concept-decomposition workflow.

Trace numbers, tables, comparisons, and figure captions to sources or run
artifacts. Distinguish proposed diagrams from observed results; disclose illustrative
or generated material where it could be mistaken for evidence. Figure/storyboard
and slide work can be delegated, with Astra owning the scientific narrative and
final interpretation. Use appropriate format tools rather than embedding office
or rendering instructions in this skill.

For venue fit or reviewer-style critique, verify current official requirements
when they affect the answer. Separate compliance issues, scientific weaknesses,
and presentation changes. A plausible reviewer concern is not an actual review;
a score forecast is not an acceptance probability. Prioritize objections that
change the validity or importance of the contribution.

For rebuttals, connect each substantive concern to a direct answer, supporting
evidence or a clearly bounded concession, and the manuscript change made or
proposed. Do not promise unrun experiments or describe proposed revisions as done.
Confidential drafts and reviews should not be sent to public search tools.

Completion means the requested artifact is usable and the relevant claims have
been reviewed against evidence. Note unresolved limits, missing experiments, or
unverified citations where they affect interpretation. For an artifact release,
check that the reported results can be regenerated from the supplied instructions
and that data, licenses, anonymity, and publication permissions match the project.
Astra makes the final judgment; an independent worker audit is useful when it
adds a genuinely different check, not as a compulsory sign-off ritual.
