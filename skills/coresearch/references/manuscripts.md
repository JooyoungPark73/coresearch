# Manuscripts, review, and release

Writing should expose the actual contribution and its evidence, not upgrade the
claim beyond the study. Work directly in the user's existing draft and format.
Preserve intended claims, author voice, deliberate organization, numbers, citations, qualifiers
and negative results. Return requested prose first, with only material diagnostics.
A supplied excerpt normally needs a local edit.

## Argument and delivery

For section or whole-paper work, make the operating context, important objective,
measured bottleneck, insight, mechanism or finding, strongest supported outcome,
and reusable lesson clear. Several connected claims can support one coherent
takeaway. State contributions as refutable claims and connect each to evidence,
scope and the section that establishes it. A short claim-to-evidence table can
help a complex draft; no separate map or state schema is required.

In ML-systems work, keep quality requirements, SLOs, model/workload assumptions,
and performance/resource/cost tradeoffs visible through the argument. Measurement
papers can center on methodology and findings without inventing a new system.
State what is proposed, implemented, simulated, tested or deployed; none of these
statuses automatically establishes an empirical result.

Use why–how–results when it clarifies the paper, adapting the structure to its
actual contribution. Each section should answer a reader question and establish
or qualify a claim:

- **Opening:** a concrete problem or finding, its context, the gap, insight and
  supported contribution. Prefer claim-to-evidence pointers over a generic roadmap.
- **Background and explanation:** the concepts needed to understand the mechanism.
  Use a concrete example before abstraction when it exposes the relevant failure;
  define terms before relying on them.
- **Design:** boundaries, interfaces, data/control paths, invariants and assumptions.
  Explain consequential choices through requirements, mechanisms, alternatives,
  rationale, tradeoffs and the evidence that tests them. Disclose implementation
  limits instead of letting the reader infer them.
- **Evaluation:** the question or claim, fair comparison, metric, controlled and
  varied conditions, observed result, uncertainty and limits. Preserve quality
  parity and measurement qualifications. Captions state the supported takeaway.
- **Related work:** represent prior systems at their strongest and compare exact
  mechanisms, assumptions, interfaces, environments or tradeoffs. Establish the
  gap early and place detailed comparisons where the reader can follow them.
- **Discussion and conclusion:** the supported lesson, limitations and transfer
  conditions. Retain failed alternatives when they explain a consequential choice.

Use direct language with concrete subjects, mechanisms and operating conditions.
Prefer a precise demonstrated problem to an inflated motivation. Introduce new
terminology only when it helps explain a recurring concept. Novelty, efficiency,
generality and causal language must retain their supporting evidence and scope,
including when compressed into an abstract or contribution bullet. Keep essential
reasoning in the main paper and distinguish exposition from discovery chronology.

When a result, assumption or claim changes, inspect every affected statement of
it, including the abstract, introduction, contributions, evaluation, captions and
conclusion as applicable. Revise those passages at the supported strength and
preserve unrelated prose. A wording-only correction stays local. If needed text
is missing, request only the passages required for the consistency check.

## Review and release

Trace numbers, comparisons, tables and captions to inspected sources or actual
run artifacts. Distinguish proposed diagrams from observed results and disclose
illustrative material where it could be mistaken for evidence. Use appropriate
format tools for figures, slides and rendering. Astra owns interpretation.

For venue critique, verify current official requirements when they affect the
answer. Separate compliance, scientific weaknesses and presentation changes.
A plausible reviewer concern is not an actual review; a score forecast is not
an acceptance probability. Prioritize validity and importance of the contribution.

For rebuttals, connect each substantive concern to an answer, supporting evidence
or a bounded concession, and the manuscript change made or proposed. Do not
promise unrun experiments or describe proposed revisions as done. Keep
confidential drafts and reviews out of public search tools.

Completion means the requested artifact is usable and its relevant claims have
been checked against evidence. Report material gaps and limits. For release,
check reproduction instructions, data and code licenses, anonymity and publication
permissions. Astra makes the final judgment; an independent audit is useful when
it supplies a different check.
